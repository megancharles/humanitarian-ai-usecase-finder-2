MOZAMBIQUE FLOOD RESPONSE - SITUATION REPORT (PLACEHOLDER / SAMPLE DATA)

Sector: Shelter, Health, WASH
Location: Zambezia Province, including Mocuba and Morrumbala districts

Overview
Heavy rainfall over the past two weeks has caused the Licungo River to overflow
its banks, displacing an estimated 18,000 people across Mocuba and Morrumbala
districts. Access roads to several rural communities remain cut off, and
responders report that they do not have reliable information on which villages
are currently reachable by road versus only by boat. Field teams have not been
able to confirm the current extent of flooding beyond what was last observed
three days ago, and there is no up-to-date estimate of how many homes remain
submerged.

Health
Three health posts in Morrumbala district have reported disruptions to
operations due to flooding, but the district health office does not have a
consolidated view of which facilities are still functional. There are also
unconfirmed reports of a rise in diarrheal disease cases in displacement sites,
but case numbers have not been systematically tracked, so the scale of the
outbreak risk is unclear.

Shelter and Camp Coordination
Four spontaneous displacement sites have formed around Mocuba town. Site
coordinators report overcrowding but do not have an accurate count of the
population at each site, making it difficult to plan latrine and water point
allocation. Partners have also noted that damage to permanent housing has not
been systematically assessed since the flooding began; initial estimates are
based only on visual observation from accessible roads, and there is no
damage assessment for communities that remain cut off.

Communication with Affected Communities
Several community leaders have asked for a reliable channel to receive
updates on flood forecasts and relocation guidance, noting that rumors are
currently spreading faster than official information in affected
communities, some of whom speak Portuguese and others local languages
including Chuwabo and Sena.

Logistics
Partners report uncertainty about which secondary roads and bridges remain
passable, which is delaying the positioning of relief supplies closer to
affected districts.
